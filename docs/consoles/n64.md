# Nintendo 64

**The N64 needs two circuits closed: 12 V and 3.3 V.** Closing only the 3.3 V
gives a console that looks on (power LED, cartridge LED) but with no video.

## How the supply works

The external brick feeds two rails through a 6-pin connector: **3.3 V
(up to 2.7 A)** and **12 V (0.8 A)**. The board makes its own 5 V from the
12 V rail, and that 5 V feeds the video side. The slide power switch gates
**both** incoming rails
([reference](https://consoleartisan.com/reference/n64/)).

## Power switch pads (8 pads, numbered 1 to 8 on the board)

| Pads | Circuit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 8 and 7 | 12 V | open when off, closed when on. Pad 8 is the supply side. |
| 5 and 6 | 3.3 V | open when off, closed when on. Pad 5 is the supply side. |

**Never connect the relay between pads 5 and 8** (3.3 V and 12 V supply
sides): that shorts two rails together.

## Wiring used

- **J4** across pads **8 and 7** (12 V), **J5** across pads **5 and 6**
  (3.3 V). Both are poles of the same relay, so they close together.
- **Buck input:** from the supply connector (always live, console off): about
  11.1 V measured on the 12 V pin, ground on a ground pin. The numbering of
  that connector differs between sources, so confirm with the multimeter
  (about 11 V on the 12 V pin, about 3.5 V on the 3.3 V pins).
- **POWER_SENSE (J6):** the switched side of the 3.3 V pair (pad 6): 0 V when
  off, about 3.5 V when on.

## Lessons learned

- With the relay closed, 7 and 8 read as connected even with the console off:
  that was the relay, not the switch. This led to wiring only the 3.3 V pair
  at first.
- With only the 3.3 V closed, the cartridge LED blinks and the sense works,
  but the HDMI mod stays dark. Closing the 12 V pair as well fixed it.
- Tested with a SummerCart64 and a Pixel FX Retro GEM.
